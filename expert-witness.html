Frequently asked questions

What does a cybersecurity expert witness do?

A cybersecurity expert witness examines digital evidence and explains, independently and in plain language, what it shows. That can mean reconstructing how an attacker entered a network, identifying the malware involved, establishing a timeline from logs, or assessing whether evidence was collected and preserved properly. The expert's duty is to the court, not to the party that appointed them.

Is Dr. Alazab registered as an expert with Qatar's Ministry of Justice?

Yes. Dr. Moutaz Alazab is a Certified Cybersecurity Expert Witness registered with the Ministry of Justice of the State of Qatar under registration number 346.

What kinds of cases involve a digital forensics expert?

Typical matters include ransomware and malware incidents, data breaches, business email compromise and online fraud, unauthorised access to systems, theft of data or intellectual property by insiders, disputes over the authenticity of emails, documents or messages, and analysis of evidence from mobile phones and cloud accounts.

Can you provide reports and testimony in Arabic and English?

Yes. Dr. Alazab works fluently in both Arabic and English, which helps when evidence, parties or counsel use different languages.

When should a lawyer or company involve a forensics expert?

As early as possible, ideally before systems are rebuilt or devices are wiped. Early involvement protects volatile evidence, keeps a defensible chain of custody, and avoids well-meaning actions that destroy the data a case will later depend on.

What makes an expert opinion credible?

Sound method, reproducible analysis and relevant expertise. Dr. Alazab's opinions draw on more than 15 years in cybersecurity, a PhD in malware detection, 100+ peer-reviewed publications and recognition among the world's top 2% of scientists by Stanford University and Elsevier.
